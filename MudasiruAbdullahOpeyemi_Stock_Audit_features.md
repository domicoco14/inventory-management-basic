# Stock Audit Features

## 1. Stock Adjustment Approval (Manager Approval Before Write-Offs)

**Explanation:** Stock adjustments (such as write-offs for damaged, expired or missing items) must go through an approval workflow before they change the stock records. An adjustment moves from Draft, to Submitted, to Approved or Refused, and only then is it Applied to inventory. Only authorized managers can approve or refuse. Reason codes are mandatory (damage, theft/shrinkage, obsolescence, counting error, expiry). High-value adjustments escalate to higher approval levels. Every step is recorded in an audit trail showing who requested it, who approved it, timestamps, before/after quantities and comments or photos. Approvers are notified automatically and can see a pending-approvals queue.

**Relevance to an inventory management system:** It prevents unauthorized or fraudulent write-offs and keeps the live stock ledger accurate. It also gives accountability, since every change to stock can be traced to a person and a reason, and it gives managers control over losses.

## 2. Corrective Action Follow-Up (Tracking That Problems Are Fixed)

**Explanation:** When an audit finds a significant variance or problem, the system automatically creates a corrective-action record linked to that finding. Each record stores the issue description, root-cause analysis, proposed action, responsible owner, target completion date and status (Open, In Progress, Verified, Closed). A verification step requires evidence that the fix worked, such as a re-count, confirmation of a process change, a photo or a follow-up audit. Follow-up reviews are scheduled (e.g., 30/60/90 days) with reminders, overdue actions are escalated, and reports show open vs. closed actions and recurring issues. Each record links back to the original audit so the history is preserved.

**Relevance to an inventory management system:** It makes sure audit findings lead to real fixes instead of being forgotten. It reduces repeated stock errors, and trend reports on recurring issues help management improve stock control processes over time.
